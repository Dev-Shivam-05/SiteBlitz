  <privacy_feasibility_and_intent_intelligence>
Make the limits of visitor intelligence explicit:

A conventional public website cannot read a visitor's personal Chrome browsing/search history. Google's Chrome history APIs are provided to extensions with explicit permissions, not ordinary website JavaScript. Do not propose covert extensions, browser fingerprinting to bypass consent, undeclared surveillance, or acquisition of private browsing histories. A website also cannot know the visitor's actual future 15 days of browser history. At most it can estimate future interest probabilistically from permitted present and past first-party signals, and must label those estimates with their uncertainty and purpose.

It is legitimate to analyse consented or otherwise lawfully collected first-party website interactions, source/UTM metadata that is actually available, requested service categories, user-supplied requirements, authorised CRM history, aggregate acquisition channels, and appropriately verified bot traffic. Referrer, geography, identity, search keywords, cross-device continuity, and attribution can be missing, approximate, blocked, aggregated, or permission-dependent. Do not promise certainty where the data cannot support it.

Propose a privacy-conscious lead-intent model: observable signals; permissible data sources; reasons for interest labels; simple interpretable rules before more complex modelling; confidence and freshness; bias and false-positive risks; human review; consent and opt-out handling; data minimisation; access control; encryption; auditability; retention; and deletion. Demonstrate how raw events become useful lead insights without revealing unrelated personal browsing activity.

Evaluate relevant laws and consent requirements based on the verified visitor jurisdictions and implementation, rather than assuming one universal legal regime. Explain any required legal review before deploying cookies, third-party tags, session replay, precise geolocation, cross-site enrichment, or behavioural profiling. Any consent-dependent analytics must degrade gracefully when permission is refused.
  </privacy_feasibility_and_intent_intelligence>

  <measurement_and_acceptance_criteria>
Define baselines before claiming improvement. Use quantified targets as goals to validate, not as results already achieved. At minimum propose the following acceptance framework:

- Comprehension: users can explain what the company does, whom it serves, its main benefit, and the next logical action after the opening section; validate with small, representative user tests rather than intuition alone.
- Functional correctness: principal navigation, service discovery, contact, lead delivery, CMS publication, permissions, and admin reporting pass repeatable end-to-end tests.
- Responsive stability: no critical overflow, overlap, missing CTA, broken navigation, unusable form, or inaccessible content at tested viewport sizes spanning approximately 320 to 1920 CSS pixels, with actual widths recorded.
- Accessibility: target WCAG 2.2 Level AA for the public website and admin workflows; provide keyboard navigation, contrast checks, focus states, labels, semantic structure, accessible motion alternatives, and manual as well as automated testing. Treat interaction-triggered animation reduction as an additional design-quality expectation.
- Core Web Vitals: target field-data good thresholds at the 75th percentile, segmented by mobile and desktop: LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 milliseconds, and CLS at or below 0.1, while stating if real-user data are not yet available.
- Motion: storyboard scenes convey a mapped service message; reduced-motion and low-powered-device modes preserve all meaning and conversion steps; animations do not block primary tasks.
- Credibility: zero invented testimonials, clients, metrics, certifications, completed projects, or unverified claims presented as fact.
- Conversion: instrument visits to service exploration, CTA interactions, enquiry starts, completions, qualified-lead rates, response times, and downstream sales outcomes; compare to a reliable baseline and avoid promising revenue figures without evidence.
- Admin trustworthiness: dashboard numbers reconcile with documented event definitions and known source data; permission checks, data freshness, error states, exports, and reporting are tested.
- Security and privacy: approved tracking basis, minimal data collection, controlled access, safe storage, retention, consent handling where applicable, and no critical vulnerabilities introduced by the redesign.

For every major feature define an owner, measurable acceptance test, evidence method, dependency, failure case, and rollback or fallback. Include usability testing scenarios for ordinary users, low-confidence visitors, assistive-technology users, mobile users, incomplete forms, slow connections, missing assets, no consent, bot traffic, and disconnected integrations.
  </measurement_and_acceptance_criteria>
